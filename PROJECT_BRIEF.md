# PhiBean Coffees — Project Brief

## Project overview
PhiBean Coffees is a mobile-first landing page for traceable, single-origin coffee from farms in the Mullayanagiri, Seethalayanagiri and Rudragiri ranges, Karnataka, India. It helps specialty roasters, cafés, importers, wholesalers, and other professional buyers explore the coffees and submit sourcing inquiries.

The site guides buyers from origin and available coffees through processing, people and place, and buying options to a focused inquiry; it also includes a conversational sourcing chatbot and search/social metadata. It is built with HTML, locally compiled Tailwind CSS, and vanilla JavaScript.

## Product goal
Create a refined, mobile-first B2B landing page for a specialty coffee producer that communicates origin, altitude, traceable farm-level sourcing, farm processing, available micro-lots, and a calm premium brand position. Help professional buyers quickly understand where the coffee comes from, how it is processed, which varieties are available, and how to request samples, purchase lots, or discuss forward contracts.

## Coffee offering and lot information

### Origin
PhiBean coffees come from farms in the Mullayanagiri, Seethalayanagiri, and Rudragiri ranges in Karnataka, India. The farms sit at **1,400–1,600 metres above sea level** beneath a natural forest canopy. Describe the role of altitude, shade, and microclimate in the coffees' character, density, and complexity without making unsupported claims about a specific lot.

Coffee is processed at the farm, allowing control over processing from cherry to green bean.

### Available varieties
- Cauvery
- S795
- Selection 9
- Chandragiri

Do not add varieties to the available-lots table unless confirmed as part of the current offering.

### Processing
Available processes are **Washed**, **Natural**, and **Honey**.

- **Washed:** Clean and structured; lets the character of the variety and origin come through.
- **Natural:** Whole-cherry dried; can emphasize sweetness, fruit character, and body.
- **Honey:** Dried with mucilage retained; can balance characteristics associated with washed and natural coffees.

Give process descriptions enough context to explain what happens to the coffee and why a buyer might compare methods. Do not imply specific flavour results for an individual lot unless verified.

### Lot-to-origin traceability
- Explain the chain from contributing farm and range to the specific harvest lot and its buyer-facing details.
- Identify a farm or range for a lot only when that attribution is confirmed; otherwise state the broader origin accurately and mark more specific detail as unavailable.
- Keep variety, process, harvest, lot size, availability, and cup information associated with the relevant lot. Do not imply every attribute is available or verified before it is confirmed.
- Share scores and tasting notes only when verified for that individual lot.
- Describe cultivation, harvest, processing, export documentation, moisture readings, or defect screening as lot records or quality checks only when those records are confirmed for the coffee being offered.
- The standard lot reference is **30 kg**, and the harvest window is **December–March**. Do not replace these with unsupported lot sizes or harvest dates.
- Add or update main-page lot rows in `data/lots.json`; the table is generated from this catalog. Each lot requires `variety`, `process`, `growerSlug`, `size`, `availability`, and `cupDetails`. Set `growerSlug` to exactly one existing profile slug in `data/growers.json`; the table links the estate name to that grower page. Do not publish unconfirmed availability, size, or cup details as facts.

### Wholesale workflow and buyer value
- Explain the buyer path as share requirements, review available lots, agree order terms, and coordinate delivery.
- Present shipping schedules, export documentation, quality checks, and delivery visibility as order-specific details to confirm with the buyer; do not promise capabilities that have not been verified.
- Buyer-facing benefits should be grounded in the available evidence: origin details, lot-level information, sample requests, seasonal contracts, and responsive direct communication.

### Farm story blocks
- Add approved grower profiles to the top-level array in `data/growers.json`. Each record needs a unique lowercase, hyphenated `slug`, `name`, `story`, `altitude` (a confirmed value or `null`), and `varieties` (an array). `tagline` and `location` are optional. To show an embedded OpenStreetMap pin on the profile, add both `latitude` and `longitude` as decimal numbers; omit both to hide the map. Example: `"latitude": 13.123456, "longitude": 75.123456`. Get the grower's approval before publishing precise estate coordinates.
- The home page creates one profile card per catalog entry. Each card links to `grower.html?slug=<slug>`, where the shared profile template renders the grower’s details and approved images. Store each grower’s images in `assets/images/growers/<slug>/` (for example, `assets/images/growers/rayarakana-estate/`). The image manifest is generated automatically in `data/grower-images.json` during `npm run build:css`; do not edit it manually.
- Each grower profile sets a unique title, description, canonical URL, social preview metadata, and structured `WebPage`/breadcrumb data from its catalog entry. `npm run build:css` also regenerates `sitemap.xml` with one URL per grower profile.
- Keep the grower-registration and estate-visit invitation cards separate from published profiles.
- Publish only grower-approved names, locations, stories, images and farm-specific details. Leave altitude as `null` and varieties as an empty array until each is confirmed.
- Until profile information is available, describe shared origin facts as applying across PhiBean farms; do not assign an individual farm, grower, practice, or lot to a range without verification.
- Obtain permission before publishing a grower’s name, story, or identifying details.

### Editing the lot table
- Add one object per lot to `data/lots.json`. Copy the example object shape, set its `growerSlug` to the single matching slug from `data/growers.json`, and enter only confirmed lot details. Unknown values should say they are available on request; do not imply an example lot is confirmed inventory.
- The main-page table builds its estate name and profile link from that slug. A missing or misspelled grower slug is treated as a catalog error rather than rendering an unlinked estate.

### Adding grower images
- Put up to 10 supported image files (`.avif`, `.gif`, `.jpeg`, `.jpg`, `.png`, `.webp`) in `assets/images/growers/<slug>/`. Subfolders are scanned too. Use descriptive filenames because each filename is used to generate the image alt text. To add captions, create a `captions.json` file in that grower's folder and map each image's path (relative to the folder) to its caption, for example: `{ "coffee-cherries.jpg": "Ripe cherries ready for harvest.", "processing/raised-beds.jpg": "Coffee drying on raised beds." }`. Only add entries for images present in that grower's folder; the build reports missing image paths or blank captions. The grower page displays the images and captions in a slider before the story; the build fails if an estate has more than 10 supported images.
- Run `npm run build:css` before publishing. Its pre-build step discovers the image files and regenerates `data/grower-images.json`, which the home and profile pages use automatically. Do not add images to `data/growers.json` or manually edit the generated manifest.

## SEO and social metadata
Public pages include a descriptive page title and meta description, canonical URL, robots directives, Open Graph and Twitter/X card metadata, and descriptive image alt text. The homepage publishes Organization, WebSite, and WebPage JSON-LD; grower profiles publish a WebPage with breadcrumb data; and the privacy page publishes WebPage data. `robots.txt` allows crawling and points to the generated sitemap, which lists the homepage, privacy page, and grower profiles.

- Lead naturally with relevant buyer terms such as **Arabica green coffee**, **specialty green coffee micro-lots**, and the verified origin **Karnataka, India** in page titles, descriptions, and visible copy.
- Homepage title: **Crafted by Altitude | Arabica Green Coffee | PhiBean Coffees**
- Keep search descriptions accurate and readable; do not repeat keywords unnaturally or promise search ranking. Search engines may ignore the `keywords` meta tag.
- Grower profile metadata and structured data are updated by JavaScript from the grower catalog. Crawlers that do not render JavaScript may only see the generic template metadata; social preview bots may also use the default image and description.
- After deployment, verify the site in Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, submit `https://vbn1.github.io/phibeancoffees/sitemap.xml`, and inspect the homepage and grower profile URLs. Crawling and inclusion are controlled by the search engines and cannot be guaranteed by metadata alone.
